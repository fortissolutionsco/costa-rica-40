Emergency & Safety Guide

Save this page offline or print it before departure. Information researched in October 2026. Verify contacts, hours, road conditions and official advisories again immediately before traveling.

Emergency? Call 911 now.

911 is Costa Rica's national emergency number for police, fire and ambulance. Do not delay a 911 call to consult this page or drive to a clinic.

☎ Call 911📍 Open Playa Pelada map

Say: “Necesito una ambulancia / policía / bomberos. Estamos en Casa Catalina, Playa Pelada, Nosara, Guanacaste. Mi ubicación es…” Then share a GPS pin or coordinates and describe the emergency.

If English is not understood, ask “¿Habla inglés?” Keep the line open, follow dispatch instructions, and send one person to meet responders if safe.

What to do: quick emergency SOPs

Medical emergency / serious injury
  1. Call 911. Share GPS location, age, symptoms, consciousness and breathing status.
  2. Follow dispatcher instructions; provide CPR or bleeding control only if trained and directed. Do not move someone with possible spinal injury unless immediate danger requires it.
  3. Send a companion to guide responders; collect passport copy, insurance and medication list if this does not delay care.
  4. One adult accompanies the patient if permitted; another updates the designated family contact and trip lead.
Rip current, drowning or beach emergency
  1. Call 911 immediately. Alert lifeguards if present. Do not enter dangerous surf for an untrained rescue.
  2. Throw flotation or use shore-based assistance; tell responders the last-seen location and time.
  3. If caught in a rip current, float, signal and swim parallel to shore when able; do not fight the current directly.
  4. Even after apparent recovery from submersion, seek urgent medical evaluation for breathing symptoms.
Fire, smoke or gas leak
  1. Evacuate, close doors behind you if safe, and call 911 from outside.
  2. Do not reenter. Count everyone at the agreed assembly point.
  3. Tell dispatch about anyone missing and any gas cylinders or hazards.
Earthquake, tsunami warning or coastal evacuation
  1. During shaking, Drop, Cover and Hold On; stay away from windows.
  2. After strong or long coastal shaking, or an official tsunami warning, move inland and uphill immediately. Do not wait to gather belongings or go to the beach to watch.
  3. Follow local CNE/911 and evacuation signs; regroup at the designated inland point when safe.
Crime, robbery or missing person
  1. For danger in progress call 911; do not resist an armed robbery.
  2. Move to safety, preserve evidence and document incident details. Notify the embassy for serious incidents affecting U.S. citizens.
  3. For a missing person, call 911 promptly with last-known location, clothing, photo and contact details; do not delay because of a presumed waiting period.
Vehicle collision or stranded traveler
  1. Check for injuries and call 911 if anyone is hurt or the road is unsafe.
  2. Do not move vehicles except as directed by police or when required to prevent immediate danger; contact the rental company and insurer.
  3. Share live GPS, vehicle details and photos if safe; avoid driving through flooded roads or rivers.

Travel SOPs and foreseeable risks

Before departure

  • Enroll in STEP; save passport copies and emergency insurance contacts offline.
  • Confirm insurance covers adventure activities, evacuation and preexisting conditions as applicable.
  • Download maps and Spanish offline translation; pack essential medications and first-aid supplies.
  • Get the villa's exact coordinates, exit plan, extinguishers and first-aid-kit location from the host.

Daily habits

  • Use buddy check-ins, particularly at beaches, trails and after dark.
  • Lock doors/windows and keep valuables out of vehicles and beach bags.
  • Stay hydrated, use sun protection, and prevent mosquito bites.
  • Use reputable drivers; avoid unfamiliar roads at night and never cross flooded routes.

Local hazards

  • Strong rip currents and changing tides, slippery rocks and unguarded beaches.
  • Road washouts, limited lighting, animals on roads and emergency-response delays.
  • Heat, dehydration, mosquito-borne illness and food-related illness.
  • Petty theft, rental break-ins, scams, and occasional violent crime.
  • Earthquakes, potential tsunami alerts, severe rain and landslides.

U.S. State Department guidance

As checked October 2026: Costa Rica is at Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution due to crime. Petty crime is common and violent crime can affect tourists. The advisory highlights deadly rip currents, rental-property theft, limited medical services outside San José and potential earthquakes. Use normal vigilance, secure the villa, avoid swimming alone, and have medical evacuation coverage.
